# Pass 0 — Historical frame

The article is written soon after the July 7 incident, while the actual depth and durability of Nationalist resistance remain uncertain.

It takes public declarations of resistance as a starting signal but explicitly refuses to treat them as sufficient proof of policy execution.

This gives the document a useful decision structure:

```text
declared direction
-> required operational system
-> observable consistency/inconsistency
-> likely trajectory
```

---

# Pass 1 — Source / argument reconstruction

## 1.1 Two competing directions are defined

The article contrasts:

```text
firm resistance
vs
compromise / retreat
```

It treats the July declarations by both parties as evidence that a firmer resistance direction has become possible.

But it also states that compromise forces remain active.

## 1.2 A declared direction is not treated as implementation

The text asks for continued observation of whether public promises are actually carried out.

The important structure is:

```text
statement
!=
implemented policy
```

This continues V1-014's stance: declaration changes belief only provisionally; later action validates it.

## 1.3 `办法是跟着方针来的`

The core article architecture connects strategic direction to a bundle of operational methods.

Resistance cannot be reduced to a military slogan. It requires a coherent system including, in the article's own political program:

- military mobilization;
- mass mobilization;
- political rights / organization;
- finance and economy;
- education;
- national united front;
- external support and internal consolidation.

Thus the article claims:

```text
policy direction
-> determines what capabilities/actions must be built
```

## 1.4 Means can contradict the stated objective

The article explicitly contrasts `抗日` language with institutions and policies that remain organized around older non-resistance patterns.

This yields a source-level consistency test:

```text
if operational methods remain aligned with old objective
then new declared objective is not yet real in system behavior
```

## 1.5 Two policy-method bundles are mapped to two futures

The argument is deliberately binary:

```text
firm resistance + matching mobilization methods
-> national liberation

compromise + old/non-resistance methods
-> national subjugation
```

This is rhetorically powerful but epistemically stronger than the evidence can warrant with certainty.

## 1.6 Strategy is treated as a system of mutually consistent choices

The most transferable architecture is not the historical eight-point program itself but the insistence that:

```text
objective
strategy
resource allocation
institutions
mobilization
metrics/behavior
```

must point in the same direction.

# Pass 3 — Reasoning reconstruction

## R1. Commitment validation

```text
public commitment
-> derive required observable actions
-> inspect whether actions occur
```

## R2. Policy-system consistency

```text
stated objective
<-> military/economic/political/organizational methods
```

Inconsistency is treated as evidence that the objective is not actually implemented.

## R3. Means-end dependency

Methods are not neutral. The article assumes that certain objectives require a corresponding capability and institutional configuration.

## R4. Scenario branching

The article creates two end-to-end scenarios from policy direction through methods to outcome.

---

# Pass 4 — Adversarial reading

1. The two-scenario framing may exclude hybrid, delayed or partially implemented strategies.
2. The claim that one bundle `一定` yields victory and the other `一定` yields defeat is too deterministic; war outcomes depend on many external variables.
3. Some proposed methods are political commitments, not empirically derived necessary conditions.
4. Later editorial notes strongly confirm the article's critique using hindsight; they must not inflate confidence in the original forecast.
5. `Means follow policy` can run both ways: existing capability/resource constraints can also limit feasible strategy.
6. A system may temporarily use inconsistent methods during transition; inconsistency is evidence of incomplete implementation, not automatic proof of bad faith.

# First-pass takeaway

The most durable candidate is not `two policies, two futures`.

It is the control rule:

```text
for any declared strategy:
1. derive what actions/capabilities must exist if it is real;
2. observe whether those conditions are being built;
3. treat persistent strategy-method inconsistency as evidence requiring belief revision.
```

The article's deterministic binary future claims remain historically important but should not be transferred as a general forecasting method.
