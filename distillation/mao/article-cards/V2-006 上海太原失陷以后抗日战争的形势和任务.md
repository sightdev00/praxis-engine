# Pass 1 — Source / argument reconstruction

## 1.1 The current state is classified as an unstable transition, not simply failure

The article says:

```text
片面抗战已不能持久
全面抗战尚未到来
-> severe transition crisis
```

This introduces a richer stage representation:

```text
old regime failing
+
new regime not yet established
```

A system can therefore be between stable configurations.

## 1.2 The article presents both unfavorable and favorable state variables

Unfavorable:

- regular-war collapse in North China / front broken in Jiangsu-Zhejiang;
- weak real support from Britain/US/France;
- Axis support for Japan;
- KMT reluctance to change political/mass-control system.

Favorable:

- expanding CCP/Eighth Route Army influence;
- some growth of mass movement;
- leftward movement of national bourgeoisie;
- reform currents inside KMT;
- growing global popular support;
- expected Soviet material support.

This is a deliberate force-balance/state-vector analysis rather than a one-signal judgment.

## 1.3 Three branching futures are explicitly defined

The article identifies:

1. partial resistance -> comprehensive resistance;
2. resistance -> surrender;
3. resistance and surrender coexist, splitting the anti-Japanese front.

This is a genuine scenario tree rather than the earlier two-outcome rhetoric.

## 1.4 Scenario likelihood is assessed through factor comparison

It claims anti-surrender factors currently have the advantage and lists several domestic/international forces supporting that assessment.

The text still lacks numerical probabilities, but the reasoning is recognizably:

```text
scenario set
-> supporting/opposing factors
-> relative-strength assessment
-> preferred forecast
```

## 1.5 Duration uncertainty is explicitly retained

The crisis:

```text
may persist longer
or
may be overcome relatively quickly
```

This is more calibrated than V1-006's earlier exact one-year forecast.

## 1.6 A concrete conditional/time-bounded forecast is introduced

The article says that if 250k–300k politically renewed troops can be built in six months to one year, a turn in the battlefield will begin to appear and this would form a military basis for strategic counteroffensive.

This should be entered into the prediction ledger rather than judged retrospectively now.

## 1.7 The `main danger` inside the party is explicitly stage-dependent

Before July 7:

```text
main danger = left closed-doorism
```

After KMT begins resistance:

```text
main danger = right capitulation/accommodation
```

This is one of the clearest operational demonstrations of a principal-risk variable changing sign after the environment changes.

## 1.8 Cooperation is paired with independent capability

The article argues for preserving the united front while maintaining independent CCP leadership/capability in army, base areas and mass work.

This creates a dual constraint:

```text
cooperate enough to preserve shared front
+
retain enough independent capability to avoid absorption
```

## 1.9 The article records concrete organizational corrections inside this new stage

Examples include:

- restoration of political-commissar system;
- restoration of political-department naming/function;
- rejection of external appointment into Eighth Route Army leadership;
- correction of accommodation tendencies in Xi'an/Longdong;
- protection of southern guerrilla strategic points.

This is important revision evidence because coalition integration had already modified institutions, and some changes were then reversed.

## 1.10 The article distinguishes preserving a base from expanding it

`保持阵地` is necessary but not sufficient; the larger purpose is to develop/expand influence and mass organization.

Thus survival and growth are separate objectives that need balance.

# Pass 4 — Adversarial reading

1. The article interprets Shanghai/Taiyuan losses as confirming the prior `partial resistance` model, but other material/military causes remain substantial.
2. Forecasting `anti-surrender factors dominate` is qualitative and may underweight elite decision power relative to mass preference.
3. The 250k–300k / six-to-twelve-month forecast is highly specific but its basis is not shown; it must not be treated as validated because later events roughly fit some narrative.
4. The predicted possibility of KMT political/organizational reform is explicitly contradicted by later editorial hindsight; this is valuable failed-forecast evidence.
5. `Who leads whom` frames coalition dynamics as binary dominance and may underrepresent negotiated, distributed or issue-specific authority.
6. Preservation of independent capability may protect learning/agency but can also reduce coalition integration; optimal degree is not derived.
7. Later notes strongly endorse the author's side of internal disputes; retrospective editorial authority can obscure unresolved 1937 uncertainty.
