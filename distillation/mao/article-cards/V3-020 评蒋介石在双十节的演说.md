# V3-020 评蒋介石在双十节的演说

> Status: first-pass complete under current protocol  
> Date: 1944-10-11  
> Document type: Xinhua political commentary / public warning / opponent-commitment audit

## A. Identification

- Canonical text: https://www.marxists.org/chinese/maozedong/marxist.org-chinese-mao-19441011.htm
- Role: a public critique of Chiang Kai-shek's 1944 Double Tenth address, testing its assurances against military deployments, prior language and promised reforms.

# Pass 0 — Historical frame

Written amid the 1944 military crisis and growing demands for political reform. The immediate decision pressure is whether assurances of no civil war and liberalization should be trusted while anti-Communist forces are deployed.

# Pass 1 — Source / argument reconstruction

The article forms a four-step argument:

```text
unanswered public problems + repeated reassurance
-> confidence crisis / policy inadequacy

past labels + current troop deployment
-> denial of civil war is not a sufficient safety signal

declared liberalization + coercive language/actions
-> demand observable fulfillment, not verbal commitment
```

It claims the speech supplies neither a political nor military answer to the anti-Japanese crisis; reads the term “traitor” as a reusable pretext for civil war; and demands that speech freedom, constitutional transition and political settlement be measured by conduct.

# Pass 2 — Evidence audit

See `../evidence/v3-020/evidence-ledger.md`. The text itself establishes the rhetorical claims and its interpretation. Numbers, secret contacts, intent, the 775,000-force assertion and the later effectiveness of the warning require independent corroboration.

# Pass 3 — Reasoning reconstruction

1. **commitment–action comparison**: compare stated restraint with deployments and enforcement;
2. **precedent audit**: use earlier labels and campaigns to assess how a present ambiguity may be operationalized;
3. **capability-over-rhetoric test**: land, confidence and promises do not substitute for policy, personnel and execution;
4. **public warning**: make a possible escalation legible before it is normalized.

# Pass 4 — Adversarial reading

The useful method can become hostile-motive mind reading. Repeated reassurance is not proof of fear; correlation between troop placement and intent needs orders, locations, missions and alternatives. Claims of secret negotiation and factional property need provenance, not rhetorical accumulation. Later civil war supports a risk warning but cannot prove every 1944 allegation or that one speech caused the outcome.

# Pass 5 — Article-local candidate mechanisms

## H1-3-20-01 — Audit a political assurance against observable commitments

Record the exact promise, force movements, legal/administrative acts, enforcement cases, exceptions and deadlines. Escalation risk rises when costly actions contradict a low-cost assurance.

- Textual confidence: high
- Empirical confidence: medium

## H1-3-20-02 — Treat enemy labels as operational-risk indicators

When a governing actor repeatedly classifies opponents as traitors, test whether the label expands legal or military authority; preserve due process and independent review before treating it as proof of intent.

- Textual confidence: high
- Empirical confidence: medium

# Pass 6 — Cross-article update

V3-014 showed public warning used alongside private signaling and de-escalation. This article sharpens the rule: a warning must separate documented action, inferred intent and forecast outcome; otherwise it may reproduce the unfalsifiable attribution it criticizes.

## Working judgment

The durable contribution is not partisan denunciation but a verification rule: political commitments require observable implementation and a time-bound, independently auditable contradiction log. The article's strongest weakness is that it sometimes turns an evidential test into a conclusion about private motives.
