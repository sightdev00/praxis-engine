# Pass 1 — Source / argument reconstruction

## 1.1 Development is attributed to internal opposition, not only external push

The article opposes a metaphysical view of development as mere increase/decrease or external repetition.

Its dialectical claim is that development is driven by opposing tendencies inside a process, while external causes act through internal conditions.

For method, the important question becomes:

```text
what relations inside the system generate or shape its change?
```

rather than only:

```text
what outside event happened?
```

## 1.2 Contradiction is claimed to be universal and continuous

The text states that contradiction exists in all processes and throughout each process from beginning to end.

This is a very strong ontological thesis. It should be recorded as source doctrine, not automatically converted into an empirically established modern law.

## 1.3 Universality is not enough; specificity is the basis of concrete analysis

The essay repeatedly insists that different processes possess different qualitative structures and that the same general formula cannot determine concrete action.

```text
general principle
-> identify the specific process
-> identify its specific relations / stages / sides
-> use a correspondingly specific method
```

This closely matches V1-013's hierarchy from general war -> revolutionary war -> Chinese revolutionary war.

## 1.4 Different contradictions require different methods

The text explicitly argues against solving qualitatively different problems with one fixed formula.

This is one of the most transferable source-level claims:

```text
problem structure differs
-> solution method must differ
```

It is also an explicit anti-template principle.

## 1.5 A complex process contains multiple linked contradictions

The essay does not say complex systems contain only one contradiction. It emphasizes that a large process contains many contradictions, each with distinct sides, relations and stages.

To understand the whole, one must study:

- each relation;
- each side of each relation;
- their mutual connections;
- their changing forms across stages.

This supports a network/relational reading before prioritization.

## 1.6 Avoid subjective, one-sided and superficial analysis

The text defines one-sidedness through concrete failures such as knowing only:

- oneself, not the opponent;
- the current state, not the future;
- favorable conditions, not difficulties;
- one party's account, not the other party's.

The method is therefore not merely `find a contradiction`; it demands bilateral/multilateral characterization before action.

## 1.7 Stages arise because relation structure and intensity change

A process can retain a deeper identity while its stages differ because:

- some contradictions intensify;
- some temporarily resolve or weaken;
- new contradictions arise;
- forms of the fundamental relation change.

Thus stage classification is relational, not merely chronological.

This strongly corroborates patterns from V1-003, V1-012, V1-015 and V1-016.

## 1.8 The article asserts that a complex process has a principal contradiction

The strongest formulation is:

```text
multiple contradictions exist
-> one is principal
-> it leads/determines/influences the others
-> identify it to find the center of action
```

The text goes further and says grasping the principal contradiction makes the other problems readily resolvable.

For Praxis Engine this is **source doctrine with high textual confidence but limited empirical confidence as a universal rule**.

V1-003 already provides a counterpressure: some outcomes depended on several jointly necessary conditions that cannot be safely collapsed into one bottleneck.

## 1.9 Principal does not mean exclusive

The article distinguishes principal from secondary contradictions rather than deleting secondary ones.

This is consistent with V1-015, where the Sino-Japanese contradiction becomes principal while domestic contradictions continue to exist.

Therefore a more faithful operational reading is:

```text
many active relations remain
+
one may currently have the greatest system-wide strategic weight
```

not:

```text
only one variable matters
```

## 1.10 Each contradiction also has a principal aspect

Within a contradiction, the two sides are said to develop unevenly; one side occupies the dominant position and largely determines the current character of the thing.

But dominance is conditional and can switch as relative force changes.

This extends earlier actor/force dynamics into a philosophical model of state change.

## 1.11 Dominance is context-dependent even when a general causal direction exists

A particularly important qualification says that although practice, productive forces and economic base are generally dominant in certain pairs, there are special historical situations in which:

- theory;
- production relations;
- political/cultural superstructure

can become the immediate decisive side.

This prevents a simple one-way hierarchy.

## 1.12 Opposition does not imply antagonism

The article explicitly distinguishes contradiction from antagonistic contradiction.

Antagonism is one form, not the universal form of contradiction.

This matters because a naive transfer could wrongly convert every disagreement or tension into a zero-sum conflict.

## 1.13 Opposing sides can depend on each other and transform under conditions

The text gives `同一性` two meanings:

1. opposing sides depend on each other inside one process;
2. under conditions they can transform into their opposites.

The transferable structural idea is conditional relation transformation, not the literal universality of every historical example.

```text

```

## R2. Specificity-first transfer

```text
general regularity
+
specific system structure
+

```

## R3. Priority weighting

```text
multiple active relations

-> prioritize accordingly
```

## R4. Within-relation dominance analysis

```text
relation A <-> B
-> assess relative strength / dominant side
-> infer current system character
-> monitor conditions for reversal
```

## R5. Stage detection by relation reconfiguration

A stage changes when the relevant contradictions, intensities, alignments or dominant sides materially change.

## R6. Method differentiation

Different problem structures require different intervention mechanisms; one formula should not be copied across qualitatively different processes.

# Pass 4 — Adversarial reading

## A1. `Every process has contradiction throughout` is an ontological thesis, not established empirical science

The text uses examples from mathematics, mechanics, chemistry, biology and society, but these analogies do not constitute a modern cross-domain proof that all change is best modeled as contradiction.

Praxis Engine must not require contradiction vocabulary for every problem.

## A2. Binary opposition can distort multi-agent or multi-variable systems

Many real systems involve:

- more than two actors;
- networks of dependencies;
- continuous variables;
- stochastic processes;
- complementarities without meaningful opposition.

Forcing dyads may erase important topology.

## A3. `One principal contradiction in every complex process` is too strong as a universal operational rule

Counterpressure already exists inside Volume I:

- V1-003 uses a conjunctive condition configuration;
- V1-008/V1-010 use core-task + support-system relations;
- V1-013 describes several conditions that can be actively created before counteroffensive.

A principal relation may be useful for attention allocation, but it is not always equivalent to a single causal bottleneck.

## A4. `Grasp it and all problems become easy` risks severe overconfidence

Even if one relation dominates system behavior:

- secondary constraints can block execution;
- multiple necessary conditions can remain unsatisfied;
- intervention can create new contradictions;
- measurement of the supposed principal relation can be wrong.

This sentence should never become a mandatory Skill rule.

## A5. Principal-aspect language can confuse description with causality

The currently dominant side may correlate with the system's observed state without being the sole causal determinant of transition.

## A6. Historical examples are theory-laden

Many examples are selected from the same revolutionary framework that the philosophy is intended to support. This creates risk of circular illustration rather than independent validation.

## A7. `Different contradictions -> different methods` is useful but underspecified

It tells us **not** to use one method everywhere, but it does not itself tell us how to infer the right method. That still requires domain evidence and mechanism identification.

# Pass 5 — Candidate mechanisms

## H1-18A — Analyze relations before labels

A system's behavior may be better understood by mapping interacting tensions/dependencies than by classifying isolated objects.

- Textual confidence: high
- Empirical confidence: medium-high
- Modern mapping: relational/system modeling

## H1-18B — Preserve specificity when transferring general rules

```text
general model
must be rewritten by
system + stage + actor + mechanism specifics
```

- Textual confidence: high
- Empirical confidence: high
- Strong cross-links: V1-003, V1-007, V1-013

## H1-18C — Do not distribute attention uniformly across all active problems

Some relations have greater current system-wide leverage/impact than others.

- Textual confidence: high
- Empirical confidence: medium
- Boundary: first establish whether the system actually admits a dominant relation rather than a conjunction of independent necessary constraints.

## H1-18D — A current priority does not erase subordinate problems

- Textual confidence: high
- Empirical confidence: high as a planning principle
- Cross-link: V1-015

## H1-18E — Priority and dominance are dynamic state variables

The principal relation/side may change when relative forces and conditions change.

- Textual confidence: high
- Empirical confidence: medium-high
- Cross-links: V1-001, V1-002, V1-012, V1-014—V1-016

## H1-18F — Conflict form must be classified; not every tension is antagonistic

- Textual confidence: high
- Empirical confidence: high
- Modern transfer value: avoid converting disagreement, trade-off or dependency into zero-sum conflict by default.

## H1-18G — Different causal structures require different intervention methods

- Textual confidence: high
- Empirical confidence: high
- Boundary: requires a separate procedure for identifying structure and validating intervention.

# Pass 6 — Cross-article update

## P2 strengthened — Stage-specific analysis

`矛盾论` strongly theorizes a pattern already visible across social, military, economic and united-front texts: the same broad process can contain materially different stages requiring re-estimation of actors, conditions and methods.

## P2 strengthened — Non-uniform priority allocation

The corpus repeatedly rejects equal effort everywhere. However, the philosophical `主要矛盾` formulation must be narrowed by earlier operational evidence:

```text
first infer causal / constraint topology
then ask whether one relation currently dominates strategic attention
```

not:

```text
assume a principal contradiction exists
then force the system into it
```

## P2 strengthened — Specific methods for specific structures

This now has support from:

- investigation practice;
- military theory;
- economic planning;
- organization correction;
- philosophical formulation.

It is a strong candidate for later G2 evaluation.

## New constraint on Praxis Engine

The phrase `抓主要矛盾` should be represented in the future Skill approximately as:

```text
1. map the active relations/constraints;
2. determine their topology and dependencies;
3. estimate which relation currently has the greatest system-wide decision impact;
4. prioritize it without dropping secondary constraints;
5. define observable conditions under which priority should change.
```

This is **our modern reconstruction**, not a quotation or literal restatement of `矛盾论`.

# First-pass conclusion

The deepest value of `矛盾论` for Praxis Engine is not the slogan `抓主要矛盾` alone.

Its more robust methodological cluster is:

```text
reality contains interacting relations
-> relations differ qualitatively
-> inspect all relevant sides
-> preserve stage/specificity
-> do not weight every relation equally
-> identify current dominance where evidence supports it
-> expect dominance and relationship form to change with conditions
-> choose method according to the specific structure
```

The corpus simultaneously supplies an important correction to the text's strongest universal formulation:

> A complex system does not always become tractable merely by finding one principal contradiction. Some problems are configuration, threshold, support-system or multiple-necessary-condition problems.

That tension should remain visible in the Volume-I synthesis rather than being harmonized away.
