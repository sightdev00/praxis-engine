## Evidence quality summary

- Strongest design evidence: fiscal capacity depends on real production; emergency self-supply is explicitly state-bound; extraction is constrained by a claimed net-benefit rule; simplification links footprint to carrying capacity.
- Strongest later support: production/self-supply expanded, personnel footprint fell, public-grain burden ratio declined from 1941 to 1942, and the base survived.
- Strongest omitted counterevidence: severe currency expansion/inflation and opium-derived revenue complicate the clean “production + tax” account.
- Main identification gap: output, extraction, trade, money creation, weather, mobilization and coercion changed together.
- Main governance gap: the same authority defines necessary expenditure, acceptable burden, public benefit, tax limits and the time to reduce extraction.

## Variant audit

The QST body, TheoryChina page image and public Chinese comparison preserve the same nine-paragraph excerpt, figures, three-stage burden model, five simplification objectives and editorial note. No material wording variant was identified.

The principal version risk is selection: readers receive only chapter one of a larger report, followed by a later success summary. This creates a provenance asymmetry—high-level principles and favorable outcomes are visible, while the full contemporary fiscal, monetary, trade and implementation record is not contained in the canonical article.

## Adversarial checkpoints

1. What share of real supply came from tax grain, unit production, trade, currency issue, debt, aid and special commodities?
2. Which expenditures were necessary, and who could contest that classification?
3. What was self-supply's net output after labor, training and governance opportunity costs?
4. How were burden and public return distributed across households, classes and regions?
5. What objective tax, nutrition or asset threshold meant “heavy but not harmful”?
6. Did the 1943—1944 preconditions occur, and by what burden measure was the forecast met?
7. How much of apparent revenue growth was nominal inflation?
8. Did simplification remove functions or transfer costs to lower levels and households?
9. When did the special-condition justification for unit production expire?
10. How should hidden/high-externality revenue alter the policy's welfare judgment?
