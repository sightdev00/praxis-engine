## Evidence quality summary

- Strongest decision-time signal: German strategic objectives in the 1942 summer campaign were not achieved; Soviet capability and organization had survived; Axis withdrawal after El Alamein was already visible.
- Strongest later confirmation: Operation Uranus, Operation Torch and the Pacific initiative shift made late 1942 a defensible multi-theater turning period; Axis regimes were ultimately defeated.
- Main timing problem: several events normally used to validate the global turning point occurred after 11-06.
- Main identifiability problem: Soviet institutional resilience, Allied material/operational pressure, Axis mistakes and local Chinese/Pacific drivers are bundled into one necessary victory chain.
- Main semantic problem: “不可战胜”“一切法西斯势力”“不远” have no explicit failure or time boundary.

## Variant audit

The QST second-edition text and public Chinese comparison retain the same date, five-paragraph order, turning-point assertion, offensive task, ideological guarantee, China-benefit claim and closing priority. No material wording variant was identified.

Publication metadata adds a small but relevant distinction: the canonical article is dated November 6, while party-history accounts place newspaper publication on November 7. Forecast scoring uses November 6 as the decision-time boundary and does not import November 8 Torch or November 19 Uranus outcomes.
