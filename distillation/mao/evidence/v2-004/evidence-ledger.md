## Evidence-quality assessment

Strengths:
- unusually clear process history of coalition formation;
- formal-declaration vs actual-consolidation distinction;
- identifies reciprocal concessions;
- diagnoses legacy-institution mismatch;
- introduces common program as coordination mechanism.

Weaknesses:
- broad causal attribution to united-front formation/rupture;
- front-line military crisis has many confounders;
- broader political reform is partly a normative objective, partly asserted as operational necessity;
- coordination cost and internal objective conflict of broad coalitions are under-modeled.

## Adversarial questions

1. Which coalition properties are necessary: breadth, common program, trust, enforcement, independent capacity, shared command?
2. Can a narrower coalition outperform a broader one because of lower coordination cost?
3. How are disagreements under a common program resolved?
4. What metrics distinguish `declared`, `operationalized`, `consolidated` and `stress-tested` coalition states?
5. How are temporary policy concessions prevented from being interpreted by core participants as permanent abandonment?
