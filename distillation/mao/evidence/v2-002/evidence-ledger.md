## Evidence-quality assessment

Strengths:

- recognizes real adversary-policy change rather than freezing earlier labels;
- distinguishes new stage from incomplete institutional transition;
- decomposes one objective into multiple capability domains;
- explicitly links methods/institutions to strategic objective.

Weaknesses:

- propaganda genre encourages imperative and deterministic wording;
- no decomposition of which ten-point components are necessary, sufficient or merely politically preferred;
- broad bundle makes causal falsification difficult;
- historical analogies (Ethiopia, USSR, Spain) are selectively interpreted;
- some observations are reports from an interested political organization.

## Adversarial questions

1. Could a government-centered strategy have succeeded under different international/material conditions?
2. Which parts of the ten-point program causally affect military effectiveness and through what pathway?
3. If comprehensive mobilization occurs but victory does not, which claim is falsified?
4. How should stage state be represented when military action has transformed but political institutions have not?
5. Are all residual old-state mechanisms blockers, or can some remain harmless during transition?
