## Evidence quality summary

- Strongest forecast evidence: Germany and Japan were on a worsening strategic path; subsequent Axis defeat confirms direction.
- Strongest governance method: compare abstract promises with prior compliance, current costly actions and a public acceptance checklist.
- Strongest counterevidence: the claim that starting civil war necessarily ends in unconditional surrender to Japan did not occur.
- Main quantitative gap: troop totals, 58/42 enemy-force allocation, defector counts and global morale claims lack published provenance in the article.
- Main causal gap: later de-escalation, democratic mobilization and conflict resulted from multiple internal, international, military and public-pressure mechanisms.

## Variant audit

The QST and Marxists versions preserve the same date, conference sequence, international assessment, three future directions, conflict history, policy demands and coalition appeal. No material body divergence was identified.

The most important audit is outcome-layer separation: 1943 easing supports the temporary-delay branch, 1944 democratic activity supports effort toward the third branch, 1944 military defeat supports some capability criticism, and 1945 Japanese surrender contradicts the predicted unconditional Kuomintang surrender. These outcomes do not collapse into one verdict.

## Adversarial checkpoints

1. What exact date would make “general solution eve” true or false?
2. Are the three paths mutually exclusive, or should they be state dimensions?
3. Which public and private records reveal motives rather than merely actions?
4. What evidence supports each force number and denominator?
5. How much did anti-Communist deployment reduce anti-Japanese capacity?
6. Which event could falsify the claim that constitutional language is only delay?
7. Are own-side promises and uses of force audited by the same standard?
8. Which acceptance tests are immediate, staged or dependent on prior steps?
9. Who verifies elections, freedoms, military command and negotiation compliance?
10. How should later civil war update the risk forecast without retroactively proving surrender or motive claims?
