## Evidence-quality assessment

Strengths:
- systematic derivation from context to rules;
- many explicit boundary conditions;
- reversible state transitions;
- clear distinctions among global/local and central/decentralized control;
- includes cautious prospective claims later auditable.

Weaknesses:
- primarily military theory and selected experience, not comparative causal study;
- several enemy-intent/weakness claims are prospective;
- later notes can create success-selection bias;
- command principles may transfer poorly to high-rights/non-hierarchical domains without additional safeguards.

## Adversarial questions

1. When does local autonomy become drift/fragmentation rather than responsiveness?
2. What bottom-up mechanism challenges a wrong centralized strategy?
3. What information must be reported upward to keep strategic coordination current?
4. Which background conditions can actually be changed vs only exploited?
5. When should a subsystem remain permanently auxiliary rather than `mature` toward centralization/regularization?
6. How should safety constraints alter local autonomy even when local information is freshest?

## Prediction audit

### Plains-base viability

- Original stance: broad plains guerrilla activity and temporary/small/seasonal bases feasible; long-term fixed base not yet proven and treated cautiously.
- Later O7 note: wartime experience confirmed long-term and often fixed bases in plains.
- Status: **partially confirmed / stronger outcome than initial cautious expectation**, subject to historical evidence audit.
