## Evidence quality summary

- Strongest forecast: Stalingrad as a major Eastern/European turning point; Soviet winter counteroffensive; Axis-alliance weakening; Japan's worsening trajectory.
- Strongest mechanism: German objective-capability mismatch and culmination under defender growth/seasonal/logistical pressure.
- Clearest error: battle victory declared before operational completion.
- Additional overreach: no later large German offensive; immediate strategic-defense death; synchronized second/third fronts; “human history” as measurable status.
- Main validation gap: decision-time intelligence set, source reliability, force replenishment estimates and probability assigned to alternative German/Soviet outcomes.

## Variant audit

The QST second-edition text and public Chinese comparison preserve the same seven-paragraph argument, phase sequence and forecast bundle. The Selected Works note only expands the Napoleon analogy. No material wording variant was identified in the two accessible public Chinese texts.

The primary identity risk is temporal rather than textual: later readers know the February 1943 outcome, while the editorial speaks on 1942-10-12. All outcome language must be reclassified against that decision date.

## Adversarial checkpoints

1. Which 10-09 report justified declaring the battle won rather than merely stabilized?
2. What reserves, logistics and alternative objectives remained to each side on 10-12?
3. What observable condition distinguishes a last struggle from a renewed capability peak?
4. At what scale and date does “turning point” become confirmed?
5. What counts as a large-scale offensive after strategic initiative is lost?
6. Which theater and force size would satisfy “second front”?
7. How independently were Axis allies able to exit, and what caused each transition?
8. Which Japan outcomes were caused by European developments versus Pacific/Chinese theaters?
9. How should morale messaging preserve uncertainty without causing paralysis?
