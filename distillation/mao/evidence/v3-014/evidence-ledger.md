## Evidence quality summary

- Strongest event evidence: the July 3 intelligence, troop movement, protest telegrams and July 9 temporary-stop order jointly show a real crisis rather than a fabricated warning.
- Strongest method: expose observable deployments and opportunity costs, demand verifiable withdrawal, split the opponent coalition and preserve a cooperation off-ramp.
- Strongest causal boundary: the temporary-stop order preceded publication; the outcome belongs to a bundle of private protest, publicity, opinion, international factors, military preparation and opponent calculation.
- Main attribution gap: behavioral similarity and a shared anti-communist incentive do not identify direct collaboration, tacit agreement or every person placed in a “fifth column” category.
- Main governance risk: punishment is demanded for an attributed propaganda organizer without publishing an adjudication path, defense right or independent evidence review.

## Variant audit

The QST body and the public Chinese comparison preserve the same central sequence: troop movement, anti-communist propaganda, comparison with enemy/pseudo-regime behavior, appeal to patriotic Kuomintang members and specific de-escalation demands. No material body divergence was identified in the accessible comparison.

The decisive variant is temporal rather than textual. The July 9 “暂不动作” order and July 10 notification preceded the July 12 editorial, while October public de-escalation and December easing followed it. These states must not be compressed into a story in which one article independently stopped an attack.

## Adversarial checkpoints

1. Which observed deployment pattern distinguishes attack preparation from coercive signaling or routine redeployment?
2. What part of the intelligence can be disclosed without exposing sources, and what independent corroboration exists?
3. Does a river-defense gap make Japanese exploitation likely enough to change the decision, or merely possible?
4. Who organized and financed the alleged cultural meeting, and can participants answer the accusation?
5. Which evidence tier supports similarity, common incentive, tacit agreement and direct collusion respectively?
6. Which named actors performed which acts, rather than inheriting a collective label?
7. What observable withdrawal, propaganda halt or negotiation would count as de-escalation?
8. When should public pressure be reduced to avoid converting deterrence into an escalation trap?
9. Which part of the July outcome is attributable to private protest, publicity, international pressure and military readiness?
10. How are false or overstated public accusations corrected after the immediate crisis passes?
