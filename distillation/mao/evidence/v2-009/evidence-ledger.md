## Evidence-quality assessment

### Strengths

- genuine pre-event claims from 1936/1937 are quoted;
- competing forecasts are explicit;
- model is multi-dimensional and dynamic;
- exact duration is deliberately left uncertain;
- stage model explicitly anticipates correction;
- local/global rules and multiple outcome dimensions are carefully distinguished;
- final text explicitly invites correction.

### Weaknesses

1. **Self-validation bias**: 1938 repeatedly claims prior views are already proven correct.
2. **Moral/causal mixing**: political-moral categories enter the causal state model.
3. **Inevitability language**: final victory and multiple future trajectories are stated more confidently than exact-time claims.
4. **Broad-model flexibility**: many favorable factors can mature at different speeds, weakening falsifiability.
5. **Retrospective loss rationalization risk**: multiple benefits from losses can become ex-post justification without pre-action baselines.
6. **Stage fit flexibility**: broad stages can be retrospectively fitted unless transition indicators are specified.

## Adversarial questions

1. What observation before final defeat/victory would have materially lowered confidence in the protracted-victory model?
2. Which variables are necessary, sufficient, compensatory, or merely supportive?
3. How should moral categories be operationalized into measurable mechanisms?
4. What exact indicators mark Stage 1 -> Stage 2 and Stage 2 -> Stage 3?
5. How much local victory accumulation is required before global transition becomes likely?
6. Which earlier forecasts in 1936/1937 were wrong or unresolved but omitted from the self-validation narrative?
7. How should stochastic shocks and third-party entry update the model?

## Prediction-audit priorities

Create/track independent predictions for:

- three-stage sequence;
- Stage 2 as longest/hardest transformation pivot;
- regional/nonuniform start of counteroffensive;
- increasing international aid;
- growing Japanese resource/economic/morale constraints;
- Japanese internal anti-war/revolutionary changes;
- eventual Chinese transition from weakness -> parity -> superiority;
- possibility/importance of continued large guerrilla warfare;
- specific Stage-1 Japanese objectives/force/time estimates.

Score direction, timing, mechanism and threshold separately.

## Cross-volume relevance

Strongly supports:

- dynamic state/trajectory model;
- stage as conditional contour;
- claim-level uncertainty;
- multi-level rule scoping;
- preservation of future action capacity / ruin avoidance;
- prediction-ledger necessity;
- continuous execution feedback.

It is a major source for eventual Bayes/Kelly integration, but those concepts remain explicitly excluded from Mao Phase-1 interpretation.
