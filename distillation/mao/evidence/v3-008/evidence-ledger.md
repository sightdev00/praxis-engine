## Evidence quality summary

- Strongest contemporaneous evidence: changed audience environment; speech's first-person account; observed language/contact problems; three-session forum and existing debates.
- Strongest operational mechanisms: audience/source coupling; field immersion; popularization-improvement loop; specialist/frontline reciprocity; issue-specific coalition; intent/effect distinction.
- Strongest counterevidence: variant history and the Wang Shiwei treatment show that classification and political campaigns shaped both text and speech freedom.
- Weakest claims: sole-source ontology; universal class/political determination of art; 90-percent representation; complete political-truth unity; organizational unity as proof of learning.
- Main validation gaps: original meeting transcript; participant-level input-to-output mapping; independent audience feedback; harm/distribution measures; appeal and error correction for political classification.

## Variant audit

The second-edition corpus text is a later policy artifact, not a verbatim 1942 transcript.

1. 1942: forum speeches were stenographically recorded and reportedly printed by Qiqi Press.
2. 1943: *Liberation Daily* and Jiefangshe published a revised text; the Central Study Committee ordered broad study and distribution.
3. 1953: the Selected Works version revised the 1943 text extensively, regularized language and theory, changed some political terms, and removed the “special-service/spy literature” passages.
4. 1991: the body text remained unchanged; several historical notes were corrected or softened.

The most material methodological variant is not punctuation but classifier governance: an accusation family added during the Rescue Campaign was later removed without the standard corpus text itself displaying the previous claim or its downstream consequences.

## Adversarial checkpoints

1. Which participant claims changed the final conclusion, and which disagreements disappeared?
2. Who measured what workers, peasants, soldiers and cadres needed or welcomed?
3. Can audience feedback be independent when publication, criticism and organization are centrally governed?
4. What evidence can reopen the protected political and class framework?
5. How are present and future interests aggregated, and what protects harmed minorities?
6. When does field immersion produce understanding, and when does it become identity conformity?
7. What transfer variables determine whether one area's popularization experience fits another?
8. Who classifies a critic as enemy/counterrevolutionary, and how can that decision be appealed and reversed?
9. How are political, artistic, factual and harm effects kept from collapsing into one political score?
10. Which later works support the audience/source mechanism without validating coercive governance?
11. How should added-then-deleted high-consequence claims remain visible in a canonical text history?
