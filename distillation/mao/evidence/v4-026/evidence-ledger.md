## Evidence quality summary

- **Strongest source evidence:** explicit policy boundaries and thresholds; correction/return of misclassified property; consent protection for wealthy middle peasants and new rich peasants; continued operation of useful businesses; few killings/no random killing; elections through people's congresses; two necessary conditions for coalition leadership.
- **Strongest observable-style evidence:** the text itself is a decision draft and states concrete operational rules. This establishes what was proposed, not whether local actors followed it.
- **Weakest empirical claims:** successful wartime cooperation with enlightened gentry, the size and political behavior of intellectual minorities, the productive effect of rich-peasant policy, and the claim that the proposed coalition would retain support.
- **Main missing measurements:** classifier denominators and error rates, local implementation variance, property-return completeness, production and welfare outcomes, court independence, appeal/remedy, and representation of non-aligned groups.

## Adversarial checkpoints

1. Can a local authority falsify the 25 percent denominator or “large surplus” while appearing to follow the rule?
2. What stops “few killings” from becoming a quota or from hiding severe non-lethal harm?
3. Who independently checks “firm counterrevolutionary,” “genuine tyrant,” “good conduct” and “popular hatred”?
4. How are wrong classifications corrected when property, livelihood, status or safety cannot be fully restored?
5. Does the one-third representation example affect actual decisions, or only supply symbolic inclusion?
6. What evidence shows that protected output also meant protected economic rights?
7. How are party leadership, people's-congress representation and exclusion of enemies reconciled when they conflict?
8. Which later policy changes are genuine empirical revisions, and which are strategic changes under new conditions?
