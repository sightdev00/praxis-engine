# P2-01 — Actor/force positions and strategy are state-dependent

## Earlier evidence

### V1-001

Different groups are predicted to move as material pressure and perceived revolutionary success change.

### V1-002

Poor, middle, and rich peasants show different observable joining behavior as peasant associations gain strength.

## New evidence from V1-003

The August defeat is explicitly explained through a state change:

```text
enemy political fragmentation
-> one strategic regime is viable

opposing forces become temporarily more stable
+ strategy appropriate to fragmentation is still used
-> defeat
```

This is materially different from social-group movement. The object is now strategic force configuration and military deployment.

## Current claim

> A strategic position or actor map should include not only current status but the environmental/force conditions under which that status or strategy is expected to remain valid.

## Confidence

`P2 — cross-context pattern`

- Textual confidence: high.
- Empirical confidence beyond the corpus: medium-low; mechanism is plausible but still under-specified.

## What would weaken it

- later military texts repeatedly recommend fixed strategy independent of state changes;
- later “stage” language proves rhetorical rather than decision-relevant;
- August defeat attribution is contradicted by stronger evidence pointing to unrelated causes.

# P2-02 — Effective force is capability-structured, not raw quantity

## Earlier evidence

### V1-001

Industrial workers are treated as important partly because of concentration and structural position, not population share alone.

### V1-002

Dispersed peasants become a qualitatively different force when organization, coordination, local leadership, and execution expand.

## New evidence from V1-003

The article explicitly distinguishes:

- popular support;
- local Red Guards;
- a regular Red Army capable of meeting regular enemy forces;
- party organizational strength and policy quality;
- terrain;
- logistics and medical support.

It states that strong popular support without a sufficiently capable regular force is not enough for a durable independent regime.

## Current claim

> Nominal quantity should not be treated as usable power without checking organization, task-matched capability, coordination, structural position, and sustainment.

## Confidence

`P2 — cross-context pattern`

This has now appeared in social, organizational-mobilization, and military-survival settings.

## Important refinement

Do not preserve the earlier pseudo-formula as if all factors simply multiply. V1-003 suggests at least some components may behave as **threshold or non-substitutable constraints** rather than smooth multipliers.

That is an important correction to our own initial abstraction.

# Major challenge to an earlier hypothesis — one enabling constraint vs condition configuration

After V1-002 we tracked:

> Find the enabling constraint before spreading effort across many tasks.

V1-003 complicates this sharply.

The survival problem is represented through several levels of conditions, including:

- macro political fragmentation;
- prior local organizational history;
- national trend;
- regular military capability;
- party organization/policy;
- economic/logistical support;
- base construction.

Some appear non-substitutable or threshold-like.

Therefore the earlier candidate should be split:

### H1-C1 — Bottleneck mode

Some problems have a dominant enabling constraint whose resolution unlocks downstream work.

### H1-C2 — Configuration mode

Some problems require a viable configuration of several conditions; searching for one “principal bottleneck” can destroy the model.

Future articles should help determine **how to decide which mode applies**.

This is the first direct example in Praxis Engine where a new article changes not just confidence but the *shape* of a candidate mechanism.

# New H1 candidates from V1-003

## H1-16 — Exceptional outcomes require context-specific explanation

When an outcome contradicts the normal expectation, investigate what is structurally unusual before treating it as a generally reproducible recipe.

## H1-17 — Match capability level to task/opponent level

Different capability types are not freely substitutable.

## H1-18 — Strategy validity is conditional on state

A strategy should carry assumptions about the environment under which it is expected to work.

## H1-19 — Analyze failure by violated assumptions

Do not stop at “the plan failed”; identify which assumed condition changed or never held.

## H1-20 — Sustainability constraints can dominate after strategic feasibility is established

A strategy may be politically/militarily possible yet operationally unsustainable because of logistics, health, supply, or infrastructure.

## H1-21 — Past organization can persist as present capability

Historical mobilization and training may leave a capability residue that changes current feasible actions.
